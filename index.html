        Sources, Assumptions & QGIS Export

        Transit Feeds

        • TransJakarta BRT, Non-BRT & Mikrotrans: Official PT Transportasi Jakarta static GTFS (gtfs.transjakarta.co.id, CC BY 4.0), merging opposite-side stops sharing mode & name within 85 m.
        • MRT Jakarta, LRT Jakarta, LRT Jabodebek, KRL & KAI: OpenStreetMap Overpass railway stations cross-checked against active station registries (excluding unbuilt corridors).
        • Border Buffer: Stops up to ~1.2 km outside DKI Jakarta's border are retained so outer Kecamatan residents still see reachable stations within a 15-minute walk.

        Service Area vs. Straight-Line Buffer

        • Live Checker: Powered by the Valhalla Pedestrian Routing Engine (valhalla1.openstreetmap.de/isochrone, OpenStreetMap street/footpath graph) at 5, 10, and 15-minute walking contours.
        • Why Service Area matters in Jakarta: Rivers/canals (Ciliwung, Banjir Kanal), toll roads, rail corridors, and superblocks block direct walking paths. Toggle Compare vs. straight-line circle to measure the Street Connectivity Ratio.
        • Citywide Kecamatan Ranking: Uses a 250 m interior point grid with a 920 m street-detour threshold (1,200 m at 4.8 km/h divided by a 1.3× Jakarta detour factor).

        Open in QGIS

        Both spatial layers in data/ are standard WGS84 GeoJSON files you can drag straight into QGIS to style or run custom network analyses:

        • data/kecamatan.geojson — 42 mainland polygons with all coverage & density attributes
        • data/stops.geojson — Complete point layer with mode, routes, and district tags
